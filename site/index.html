What it does

Three parts, and nothing else

LucidDream is small on purpose. It holds your sounds, runs your plan, and records the result. It does not interpret anything, and it does not decide anything for you.

A library you assemble

Signals are short audio files — bundled with the app, fetched from a URL, or picked off your device. Scripts are small text documents that say what to play and when. Anything you pull from a URL can be saved for offline use.

A night in three phases

Pre-sleep Training, Early Sleep, and Wake Up. Each phase takes one script, or none. Training is optional; the two sleep phases cannot both be empty. Your selections are remembered between launches.

A log you can actually read

Every playback, every volume change, every reading the script branched on, timestamped to the second. One run produces one log. You can read it in the app or export the file and keep it.

How a night works

Four things you do

  1. Choose your scripts

    Pick a script for each phase you want to use, and leave the rest empty.

  2. Set the level by ear

    Move the master volume slider and press Test. It plays the script's first signal at that level, so you set it with your own ears rather than guessing.

  3. Press Start and put the phone down

    The app validates the scripts, resolves every sound, takes a wake lock and shows a notification with the current step and a Stop action. The screen can go off.

  4. Stop it in the morning, and read the log

    Press Stop — or let the script finish on its own — and the run appears in the Log tab as a timeline you can scroll through and share.

Scripts

A script is a text file you can read out loud

No visual editor, no hidden state. A script is YAML — a list of instructions, executed in order — which means you can write one in any editor, keep it in a file, mail it to someone, and read it back a year later and still know what it does. 

name: Chime through the early hours
version: 1
volume: 0.4              # master level for this script

body:
  - log: "run started"
  - wait: 90m            # let the first sleep cycle pass

  - repeat: 6
    body:
      - play: chime
      - wait: 20m

Loops, conditions, scoped volume changes and markers are all available. The full script format — with every statement, every condition, and the scripts already published here — is one page away.

Why an owl

Awake while you are not

An owl is nocturnal, hears far more than it sees, and waits without hurrying. That is exactly the posture this app takes: awake through the night, listening, patient, doing nothing at all until the moment it was asked to act.

The owl is also Athena's companion — the old emblem of attention and good judgment rather than of magic or prophecy. That is the register we want. LucidDream pays attention and keeps an honest record. It does not claim to know what your night meant.

And this particular owl was painted by hand, by a person, which is the truest thing about the project's character.

Plainly

What this is, and what it is not

  • This is a beta. Version 0.5.0 is an early build, handed out to a small group. Expect rough edges, and expect things to change.
  • The browser build is the browser build. Browsers throttle tabs that are not in front. It is not equivalent to the Android foreground service and it is not a promise of unattended overnight playback.
  • It is not a medical device. LucidDream is an experimental self-study tool. It makes no sleep-health claims and offers no clinical guidance. If sleep is causing you trouble, talk to a doctor rather than to an app.
  • A night with nothing recalled is still data. The log records what happened regardless of what you remember, and that record is the point.
  • Nothing you do here leaves your device. No account, no server, no tracking — the details are here.
